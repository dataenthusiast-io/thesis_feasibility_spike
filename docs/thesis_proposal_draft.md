# Master Thesis Draft Proposal

**Working title.** Self-Improving Language Model Pipelines for Conversational Intent Classification under Distribution Shift

**Basis.** This thesis builds on the Transfer-Projekt "Large Language Models for the Analysis of Unstructured Conversational Data," which designed and validated a zero-shot LLM pipeline for intent classification of airline customer dialogues (macro F1 = 0.993, schema failure rate 0.0%) and identified architecture economics, prompt robustness, and scalability as open questions. Up to 30% of the Transfer-Projekt is formally reusable and covers the literature foundation, the pipeline architecture, the cost model, and the AirDialogue corpus preparation. A completed feasibility study (two spike runs, fully local) validated the experimental machinery end to end and informs the design decisions below with measured evidence.

**Synopsis.** Conversational data is growing rapidly, and enterprises must convert it into insights under constrained resources: budget, latency, governance capacity, and engineering attention. Production adds a further constraint: input distributions shift over time, and static systems degrade silently. This thesis investigates whether a closed prompt-optimization loop enables language model pipelines to retain their classification quality under distribution shift without human intervention or new labels. The design proceeds in three stages: establishing a quality anchor on clean data, systematic degradation through realistic synthetic shift, and closed-loop re-optimization on the shifted data. Model scale and optimization effects on clean data are measured as supporting structure; the primary object of study is quality retention.

## 1 Problem and Motivation

**(1) Conversational data is growing rapidly.** Chat, voice, and messaging interfaces make an increasing share of enterprise interaction data dialogic; industry estimates place unstructured data at 70 to 90% of enterprise data, growing substantially faster than structured data.

**(2) Enterprises must analyze it.** At enterprise volumes, manual analysis is infeasible; unanalyzed conversational data represents unrealized insight into customer intent, friction, and demand.

**(3) LLM pipelines make the analysis feasible.** The Transfer-Projekt demonstrated reliable conversion of conversations into analyzable structure; the feasibility study reproduced this with a 3B local model (macro F1 0.997) at zero marginal cost.

**(4) But enterprise resources are constrained in every dimension.** API inference cost scales linearly with volume regardless of falling unit prices; latency budgets constrain synchronous use; EU AI Act obligations and data residency requirements bind governance capacity; and engineering attention is consumed by manual prompt maintenance, which peer-reviewed work identifies as a genuine reliability problem rather than an inconvenience.

**(5) Production data does not stand still, and static configurations degrade silently.** Real input drifts in register, phrasing, and topic mix. The feasibility study quantified this: realistic, meaning-preserving rephrasing of test utterances into casual, indirect register cost 11 to 20 macro F1 points across corpora and configurations. Notably, a prompt optimized on clean data degraded more than a manually written prompt and ended below it, indicating that static optimization overfits the clean distribution. Conventional remediation through relabeling, retraining, or manual prompt repair is slow and expensive.

**(6) A promising resolution: self-improving pipelines.** Automatic prompt optimization (APO) systematizes prompt engineering and, operated as a closed loop, may re-adapt a pipeline to shifted data without new labels or human intervention, since meaning-preserving shift leaves existing labels valid. Whether closed-loop re-optimization actually retains quality under shift, at what cost, and across which model scales, is an open empirical question and the subject of this thesis.

## 2 Research Aim

Quantify, in a factorial comparative experiment, the extent to which a closed prompt-optimization loop retains classification quality under distribution shift, compared to static configurations (manual and optimized-then-frozen prompts) and across model scales, including the cost of re-optimization.

**Guiding question:** To what extent can closed-loop prompt re-optimization retain the classification quality of language model pipelines under distribution shift, without human intervention or new labels?

**Primary measure:** quality retention per cell, defined as the ratio of shifted-data macro F1 to the clean-data anchor of the same cell, complemented by the recovery delta of the adaptive regime over the frozen regime on identical shifted data. Anchor convention: the adaptive regime has no clean cell of its own (3.1); its retention ratio uses the optimized-static clean anchor of the same model, the artifact lineage it continues. This convention is conservative, since it holds the adaptive regime to the higher optimized anchor. Absolute shifted macro F1 is co-reported for every cell, and any case where the ratio ordering and the absolute ordering of regimes diverge is analyzed explicitly, since the two answer different questions: retention of a configuration's own peak versus best quality under shift. Clean-data optimization lift and the quality-cost frontier are secondary measures.

**Scope of the claim:** the guiding question is answered for meaning-preserving form shift, under which existing labels remain valid (3.3). The design measures the adaptation mechanism, not the production operationalization of the loop (3.7).

## 3 Experimental Design

### 3.1 Factors and conditions

**Factor 1 — Model:** TF-IDF + SVM (classical static baseline, trained rather than prompted) | local SLM in three size tiers (~1B, ~3B, ~7-8B) | frontier API model (upper anchor).

**Factor 2 — Prompt regime** (language model conditions only): manual-static (expert-written prompt, frozen) | optimized-static (optimized via APO on clean data, then frozen) | optimized-adaptive (re-optimized by the closed loop on shifted development data).

**Data condition:** clean | shifted (LLM-synthetic form shift at graded severity, see 3.3).

| Model | manual-static | optimized-static | optimized-adaptive |
|---|---|---|---|
| TF-IDF + SVM | trained-static: clean + shifted | n/a (not prompted) | n/a (cannot adapt without new labels) |
| SLM ~1B | clean + shifted | clean + shifted | shifted |
| SLM ~3B | clean + shifted | clean + shifted | shifted |
| SLM ~7-8B | clean + shifted | clean + shifted | shifted |
| Frontier API | clean + shifted | clean + shifted | shifted |

The adaptive regime exists only under shift by construction; on clean data it coincides with optimized-static; its retention anchor convention is fixed in Section 2. The re-optimized programs are additionally evaluated on the clean test set (Step 8) to measure whether adaptation to shifted data sacrifices clean-data quality. The classical baseline forms its own regime, trained-static: trained once on the clean training split, then frozen and evaluated on clean and shifted test data; it participates in H1 alongside the static prompt regimes. Optimizer configuration (proposer model strength, demonstration policy) is a controlled variable: the feasibility study measured zero lift under the naive default configuration and +2.7 points under a corrected configuration on the identical task, so configuration is fixed per condition, documented, and reported.

### 3.2 Corpus selection (data-driven)

The feasibility study measured both candidate corpora under identical conditions and grounds the selection empirically:

| | AirDialogue (3 classes) | Banking77 (77 classes) |
|---|---|---|
| clean macro F1, 3B / 1B | 0.997 / 0.975 (saturated) | 0.518 / 0.267 (headroom) |
| APO lift on clean data | +0.000 (nothing to gain) | +0.027 (configuration-dependent) |
| under synthetic shift, 3B | 0.884 | 0.370 |

AirDialogue is saturated across the entire planned SLM range: the prompt-regime factor collapses (optimization finds nothing) and scale effects vanish, confirming quantitatively the ceiling limitation named in the Transfer-Projekt. **Banking77** (Casanueva et al. 2020; 77 fine-grained, semantically overlapping intents from real banking queries) restores headroom, scale separation, measurable optimization effects, and shift sensitivity, and structurally mirrors the deep, blurry taxonomies of enterprise customer-service practice. The choice is structural, not vertical: the corpus is selected for its taxonomy properties, and both corpora's measurements appear in the thesis as the documented selection evidence. AirDialogue additionally remains the low-complexity control anchor connecting the thesis to the Transfer-Projekt baseline. Its role is fixed as a minimal control slice, rerun on the rebuilt experimental harness: manual-static regime only, two SLM tiers, clean plus one shift severity. This reproduces the saturation anchor on thesis-grade infrastructure without doubling the grid.

**Benchmark contamination (limitation, bounded by design).** Banking77 has been public since 2020, and its texts and labels are plausibly present in the pretraining data of every candidate model. Clean anchors may therefore be partly inflated by memorization, and synthetic rewriting breaks the memorized surface form, so a portion of measured degradation may reflect lost contamination advantage rather than form-shift sensitivity. This cannot be eliminated, but it is bounded and reported: (a) a contamination probe (verbatim-completion and n-gram-overlap checks of test items) is run per task model before anchoring (Step 5) and exposure is reported; (b) the effect direction is stated openly: contamination inflates H1 effect sizes, which are interpreted with this caveat, while it is conservative for H3, since re-optimization cannot restore a memorization advantage; regime comparisons on shifted data are largely unaffected because contamination operates at the model level, not the regime level (contamination literature in L7).

### 3.3 Distribution shift instrument

Shift is generated as **LLM-synthetic form shift**: every evaluation utterance is rewritten by a generator model into casual, indirect, colloquial register under a parameterized severity ladder (degree of indirection and register distance), deterministic under seed. The feasibility study validated the instrument: it degrades quality more than maximal character-level corruption while remaining realistic and format-clean, and the induced loss is prompt-shaped, hence in principle recoverable by prompt-level adaptation. Parametric corruption (typos, ASR-style noise) measured primarily information destruction, which no prompt can restore; it is retained only as an optional secondary severity axis with labels preserved by construction. The instrument operationalizes one member of the shift taxonomy: meaning-preserving covariate shift of surface form. Label shift, concept drift, and topic drift are not modeled, and all conclusions are scoped to this shift class. Each severity level carries a manipulation check: an independent quantitative signal of shift strength (embedding distance to the original and a judge-scored register and indirection rating), reported alongside the quality curves, so the severity ladder is evidenced as monotone rather than assumed from its prompt wording.

**Label-validity protocol (first-class method element).** Synthetic rewriting places an LLM inside the measurement instrument, and the feasibility study documented occasional label drift (rewrites that blur or flip the intent) as well as invented content (rewrites adding factual details, turning form shift partly topical). Countermeasures, fixed in advance: (a) strict family separation between shift generator, task models, and validator; (b) LLM-as-judge validation over the full rewritten evaluation set by a frontier model from a third model family, on two criteria per pair: label inferability (a competent human reader could still assign the original intent from the rewrite) and content fidelity (no invented facts or added scenarios; the deliberate omission of explicit action naming is a designed property of high-severity rewrites, not a violation), with both flag rates reported (methodological basis: the LLM-as-judge literature). The criteria are worded so the judge does not systematically flag the strongest high-severity rewrites, which would otherwise compress the severity ladder through regeneration pressure; (c) flagged pairs are regenerated under a bounded number of attempts rather than excluded where possible; exclusion rates are reported per severity level and per class, since unbounded exclusion would filter out the hardest rewrites and bias high-severity cells downward, and test sets are identity-matched across severities (the same original utterance appears at every severity level) so severity comparisons are paired; an utterance that terminally fails validation at any severity is excluded from all severity levels, preserving the pairing; (d) a small human-checked anchor set against which the judge's verdicts are themselves verified once, so the validity of the validator is evidenced rather than assumed.

**Assumption of the closed loop (stated in advance).** The adaptive regime presupposes a development set that is representative of the shifted inputs and validly labeled. The design obtains this by construction: already-labeled examples are rewritten under meaning preservation, so the original labels remain valid and no new labels are created. In production, form drift makes existing labeled sets unrepresentative of live traffic, so an operational perpetual loop would additionally require a labeling mechanism for fresh drifted samples (human, weak supervision, or judge-based pseudo-labeling). That operationalization question is out of scope (3.7): the thesis answers the mechanism question, whether shift-induced loss is prompt-shaped and recoverable by re-optimization given valid labels on shifted inputs.

### 3.4 Procedure

**Step 1 — Harness.** The feasibility-study harness (unified model interface for API and local inference, full call logging, per-condition metrics, seeded determinism) is consolidated into the experimental platform; the Transfer-Projekt pipeline provides the classification and evaluation core. Frontier API cells are reproducible in procedure but not bit-exact (hosted models do not guarantee determinism at temperature 0); repeated runs are reported in place of seeds for these cells. The frontier model version is pinned (snapshot identifier and access dates documented) to control provider-side model drift over the project period; a projected call budget for the frontier cells is fixed in the Exposé.

**Step 2 — Data preparation.** Stratified development and test splits per corpus, with test sets sized for statistical power (1,000+ examples per cell where the corpus permits) and an intent-bearing-content criterion in utterance derivation (the feasibility study showed naive dialogue slicing injects label noise that masquerades as model unreliability). Development sets are sized for optimizer label-space coverage (Banking77: 5 to 10 examples per class, stratified), addressing the feasibility study's observed coverage bound of 2 per class.

**Step 3 — Manual prompts.** Expert prompts written per model family under a documented protocol (fixed iteration budget, fixed development data), then frozen. Freezing occurs before any shifted data or shifted results are seen (pre-registration discipline): since the prompts are authored by the experimenter, who knows the hypotheses, this neutralizes the experimenter-degrees-of-freedom concern.

**Step 4 — Clean optimization.** Each LM condition is optimized with MIPROv2 on the clean development set under a fixed, logged budget and the documented optimizer configuration; the optimization target is per-example accuracy on the class-balanced development set as the example-level proxy for macro F1 (MIPROv2 requires a per-example metric; macro F1 is set-level and does not decompose per example), and the proxy relationship is stated with the results; results frozen as optimized-static. MIPROv2 is the optimizer of record: the peer-reviewed reference method (EMNLP 2024) of the framework the pipeline is built on, budget-controlled and reproducible; newer optimizers reporting larger gains (GEPA, L4) do not replace it but are addressed by an optional robustness check (Step 8). Optimization cost recorded as a first-class measurement. 3 to 5 optimizer seeds per condition; deltas reported with variance (feasibility-study run-to-run variance was approximately ±1 point). If observed deltas remain within seed variance, the seed count is increased for the affected local-model cells before conclusions are drawn (local calls carry no marginal cost; only frontier cells pay per run).

**Step 5 — Clean anchors.** All cells evaluated on the clean held-out test set, establishing the per-cell quality anchor and the definitive task-specific SLM comparison. Beforehand, the contamination probe (verbatim completion, n-gram overlap against test items) is executed per task model and exposure reported alongside the anchors (3.2).

**Step 6 — Shift generation and validation.** Synthetic form shift applied to evaluation and development data per severity level; label-validity protocol and severity manipulation check (3.3) executed; validated shifted sets frozen.

**Step 7 — Static retention measurement.** Manual-static and optimized-static cells re-evaluated on shifted test sets without modification; retention ratios computed per cell and severity.

**Step 8 — Adaptive re-optimization.** The optimizer re-runs on the shifted development set (original labels, shifted inputs, no human involvement), per condition and seed; evaluated on shifted test data. Re-optimization starts fresh per condition (full MIPROv2 re-compilation) rather than warm-starting from the frozen program; this is the framework-native notion of re-compilation and keeps optimization budgets comparable across regimes. To avoid the matched-distribution best case, recovery is additionally tested against a held-out shift variant (different generator or instruction) than the one adapted to. The re-optimized programs are also evaluated on the clean test set, measuring whether adaptation to shifted data sacrifices clean-data quality. Re-optimization cost recorded. As an optional robustness check, contingent on the Phase 5 budget review, the H3-critical adaptive cells are re-run under GEPA (L4) to test whether the retention findings are optimizer-specific.

**Step 9 — Analysis.** Retention curves per model scale and regime across severity; per-cell structured-output validity co-reported, with retention decomposed into validity times conditional accuracy (accuracy over valid outputs), separating format failure from classification failure, essential at the smallest tier and directly relevant to H3 since format failures are prompt-addressable; significance testing per cell pair (bootstrap over test examples, variance over optimizer seeds, Holm correction for multiple comparisons across cell pairs); the interaction analyses (optimization lift by scale on clean data; regime by data condition, which carries the H2 test as a difference-in-differences of the optimization lift between clean and shifted conditions); quality-cost frontier with the extended TCO model (self-hosting, optimization, and judge-validation cost components).

### 3.5 Hypotheses (draft)

- **H1 (degradation):** Distribution shift significantly reduces macro F1 in all static configurations, across model scales, including the trained classical baseline.
- **H2 (brittleness of static optimization):** The clean-data advantage of optimized-static over manual-static does not survive distribution shift: the optimization lift shrinks significantly between the clean and shifted conditions (regime-by-data-condition interaction). Prompts optimized on clean data overfit the clean distribution. (Feasibility-study evidence: a lift of +2.7 points on clean data turned into -4.1 points under shift, the clean-optimized prompt ending below the manual prompt.)
- **H3 (retention through adaptation, core):** Optimized-adaptive retains significantly more of the clean anchor than both static regimes, at a measurable and reportable re-optimization cost, without new labels or human intervention. (The feasibility study's single minimal-budget cell showed recovery over the frozen prompt, +1.0 points, but remained below the manual prompt; whether adequate budgets and repeated seeds close that gap is exactly the open question this hypothesis answers. Under the anchor convention of Section 2, the comparison against manual-static is conservative.)

Secondary expectations: clean-data optimization lift is positive and inversely related to model scale; quality per cost unit decreases markedly above the small-model tier. All outcomes are informative: documented instability of LLM-driven optimization loops means quantified limits of the adaptive regime constitute a finding in their own right.

### 3.6 Model selection

Criteria: open weights; quantized operation on commodity GPU (≤16 GB VRAM, a bound grounded in demonstrated 4-bit deployment footprints: AWQ runs on an 8 GB edge device); three distinct size tiers; documented instruction-following and structured-output capability; workable license. Primary task ladder: Qwen 2.5 1.5B (tiny), Qwen 2.5 3B (small), Qwen 2.5 7B (mid): a fully family-controlled scale ladder, removing the family confound from the scale comparison entirely. Llama 3.2 1B is retained as a single cross-family robustness cell at the tier where models differ most, connecting the grid to the feasibility study's tiny-tier measurements. Alternates if a primary fails the clean-anchor evaluation: Gemma 2 2B, Llama 3.1 8B. The feasibility study already provides task-specific measurements (Qwen 2.5 3B: 0.518 clean Banking77; Llama 3.2 1B: 0.267, with structured-output validity becoming a live variable at that scale); the clean-anchor evaluation (Step 5) completes the definitive selection.

The family-separation constraint from 3.3 is realized by an explicit role-to-family allocation:

| Role | Family (candidate) | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Task models | Alibaba/Qwen (1.5B, 3B, 7B), OpenAI (frontier anchor), plus one Meta (1B) cross-family robustness cell | Ladder above; frontier API model as upper anchor |
| Shift generator | Mistral | Distinct from all task families; instrument quality evidenced by the severity manipulation check (3.3) rather than assumed |
| Optimizer proposer | Microsoft (Phi-4 14B) | Distinct family; proposer strength is decisive (the feasibility study measured zero lift under a weak proposer), so the choice is verified in a small pilot and documented |
| Judge (label and content validity) | Anthropic (frontier) | Third frontier family, distinct from generator and task models; the role whose quality guards the measurement instrument itself |
| Held-out shift variant (Step 8) | Second instruction under the same generator, or a Gemma-family generator | Tests recovery beyond the matched distribution |

The feasibility study used a Llama model as both shift generator and proposer; this reallocation removes that coupling (its logged assumption 6). The generator change does not affect evidence reuse: all shifted data is regenerated and recalibrated on the rebuilt harness, and the feasibility-study numbers enter the thesis as design evidence, not as result cells. The proposer was reassigned from DeepSeek to Phi-4 14B after a lineage check: the locally deployable DeepSeek-R1 variants are distills of Llama 3.1 and Qwen 2.5 bases (the task families), which would silently break the family separation, and the undistilled DeepSeek models are 600B-class MoE systems outside the local-deployment scope. Phi-4 14B is family-distinct and runs at 4-bit within the 16 GB bound; Phi-4-mini consequently leaves the task-alternate list.

### 3.7 Implementation scope

**Exists (from Transfer-Projekt and feasibility study):** classification and evaluation core, unified model interface, seeded logging harness, corpus preparation for both corpora, baseline perturbation module, initial MIPROv2 integration incl. resolved tooling constraints.

**To be built:** severity-parameterized shift generator with the label-validity protocol (LLM-as-judge integration, anchor-set verification); classical baseline training; multi-seed optimization orchestration and budget accounting; TCO model extension; statistical analysis layer.

**Deliberately out of scope:** fine-tuning of the SLMs (the research question concerns prompt-level adaptation; weight-level tuning would confound the comparison, expand scope, and reintroduce exactly the labeling and retraining cost the closed loop avoids); drift detection and adaptation triggering (the experiment triggers re-optimization exogenously; when a production system should adapt is the subject of the self-adaptive-systems literature, cf. Casimiro et al. 2024 in L6); the production operationalization of adaptation labeling (the loop is evaluated given a validly labeled shifted development set, which the synthetic instrument provides by construction, see 3.3); proprietary production data; multi-turn dialogue state tracking.
